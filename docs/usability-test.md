# InterviewOS usability test

A 30-minute moderated session with one participant. Run it with 3–5 people who are preparing for (or recently had) tech interviews. Five sessions find most of the big problems; stop early if the same issues keep coming up.

## Setup (before each session)

- Run the app locally (see README), or share a staging link. Use a fresh browser profile so the participant starts signed out.
- Screen share plus think-aloud. Record only with consent.
- The moderator reads the tasks word for word and doesn't help. If someone is stuck for 2 minutes, note it, then give the smallest hint that unblocks them ("What did you expect to happen?").
- Keep a copy of the note sheet at the end per participant.

## Intro (read aloud, 2 min)

> "We're testing the product, not you. There are no wrong answers. Please think aloud: say what you're looking at, what you expect, and anything that surprises or annoys you. I can't answer questions during the tasks, but I'll note them and we'll talk at the end."

Warm-up questions:
1. When is your next interview, and for what kind of role?
2. How do you practise for interviews today?

## Tasks

Time each task and mark it: ✅ done unaided · 🟡 done with a hint · ❌ not done.

| # | Task (read aloud) | Success looks like | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "You've just found this site. In your own words, what is it for?" (landing page, 1 min, no clicking) | Mentions practice interviews + feedback + improving over time | Do VERA/ARIA make sense? Does the 3D room help or distract? |
| 2 | "Create an account and set yourself up for the job you want." | Registers (login card switches to sign-up), finishes the profile wizard | Do they find "Create account"? Any wizard step confusing? |
| 3 | "Start a short practice interview for your role." | Picks a preset (or customises), presses "Take the seat" | Do the preset cards read as choices? Is VERA's brief read? |
| 4 | "Answer the first question as you would in a real interview. If you're stuck, get help from the app." | Types an answer, finds Hint, submits (button or ⌘/Ctrl+Enter) | Is the timer stressful or useful? Is the hint found? |
| 5 | "What did the interviewer think of that answer?" | Reads the score ring, what worked / to improve | Is the score understood (x/10, tier)? Is the feedback trusted? |
| 6 | "Finish the interview and find out how you did overall." | Reaches the report, reads the headline and summary | Do they scroll through the chapters? What do they skip? |
| 7 | "Based on the report, what should you practise next, and how would you start?" | Uses "Practise weak areas" / the drill card, or asks ARIA | Is the drill connected to the weak areas in their mind? |
| 8 | "Ask the mentor why you lost marks, and check where her answer comes from." | Asks ARIA, hovers/taps a citation chip, opens the cited report | Do they notice or trust citations? |
| 9 | "You have an interview at a specific company in three weeks. Get a plan." | Uses "Prepare for a company" (or types it), reads the plan | Is waiting OK with the step timeline? Are week buttons understood? |
| 10 | "Go back to where you can see all your progress." | Reaches the desk (dashboard), reads trend / topics | Is the 3D desk understood? Are the report sheets clicked? |
| 11 (optional) | "Try a coding interview." | Writes code, runs examples, submits with an approach | Editor comfort, reading test results, the approach box |

## Wrap-up questions (5 min)

1. On a scale of 1–5, how likely would you be to use this to prepare? Why?
2. What was the most useful moment? The most confusing?
3. Did VERA's questions feel like a real interview? Did the scores feel fair?
4. Was anything too slow, too flashy, or hard to read?
5. What's missing that would make you come back tomorrow?

## After all sessions

- Group problems by task and count how many participants hit each one. Anything that 2+ people hit at severity 1–2 goes into the next phase's backlog.
- Compare task 5 and 6 answers with the actual scores: if people misread the scores, fix the words before touching the visuals.
